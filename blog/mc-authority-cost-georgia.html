How Much Does an MC Authority Cost in Georgia?

Published: February 20, 2026

Starting a trucking company in Georgia is a great opportunity, but one of the first questions new owner-operators ask is: "How much does it actually cost to get my MC Authority?"

The short answer is that the federal filing fees are relatively low, but the total true cost depends heavily on insurance, truck registration, and state-specific taxes. We've broken down every expense you will face when establishing your Georgia motor carrier authority.

1. The Federal FMCSA Filing Fees (Fixed Costs)

Before you haul a single load, you must pay the federal government to establish your legal operating authority.

  • USDOT Number: $0 (Free)
  • MC Number Filing Fee: $300 (Paid to the FMCSA)
  • BOC-3 Filing (Process Agents): $50 - $100
  • UCR (Unified Carrier Registration): $37 to $46 (for 1-2 trucks)

Subtotal for Federal Fees: ~$400 - $450

2. The Georgia Intrastate vs. Interstate Choice

If you plan to only haul loads within the borders of Georgia (Intrastate), you do not need an MC Number; however, you will need a Georgia Intrastate Motor Carrier (GIMC) registration.

If you plan to haul freight across state lines (Interstate), you need both your USDOT and MC Numbers. For interstate travel, you must also register for the International Registration Plan (IRP) and the International Fuel Tax Agreement (IFTA).

3. Georgia Apportioned Plates (IRP)

Georgia requires apportioned plates for trucks over 26,000 lbs crossing state lines. IRP costs are calculated based on the states you travel in and the weight of your truck.

For an 80,000 lb truck running regionally in the Southeast, you can expect your initial Georgia IRP registration to cost between $1,200 and $1,800 annually.

4. IRS Form 2290 (Heavy Highway Vehicle Use Tax)

If your truck weighs 55,000 lbs or more, the IRS requires you to pay the HVUT before the Georgia Department of Revenue will issue your IRP plates.

The cost is $550 per year for an 80,000 lb truck, pro-rated depending on the month you first put the truck on the road. If you need this filed instantly to get your tags, Jayboi Services files 2290s with a 2-hour guarantee.

5. The Big Expense: Commercial Truck Insurance

This is where the costs jump. The FMCSA requires a minimum of $750,000 in Auto Liability to activate your MC Number, but almost every broker requires $1,000,000 in Auto Liability and $100,000 in Cargo Insurance.

For a new venture in Georgia, down payments for commercial insurance usually range from $1,500 to $4,000, with total annual premiums ranging from $12,000 to $25,000+ depending on your driving record and credit score.

Summary: Your Total Out-of-Pocket Cost

To go from zero to actively hauling freight across state lines in Georgia, you should have at least $3,500 to $6,500 saved up just for the legal setup, plates, taxes, and insurance down payment.
